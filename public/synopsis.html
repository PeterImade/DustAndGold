On Becoming: Dust & Gold

On Becoming: Dust & Gold is a literary memoir about the lifelong work of becoming human in a world that often wounds us before we understand ourselves. Moving between autobiography, philosophy, psychology, history, theology, literature, and cultural criticism, Otito Nosike explores the invisible forces that shape identity: love and abandonment, hope and disappointment, suffering and resilience, family, memory, faith, shame, power, and the stories we inherit long before we learn to question them.

Beginning in the intimate moments of childhood and unfolding through adulthood, the book follows the author’s encounters with grief, first love, betrayal, lack, education, religion, mortality, and the difficult work of forgiveness. Every experience becomes a doorway into larger questions about human nature.

Why do people hurt one another? What becomes of pain when it is left unattended? Why do societies repeat their mistakes? What does hope demand of us? Why does love do to the human condition? What does it truly mean to become?

Drawing from thinkers as varied as Viktor Frankl, Fyodor Dostoevsky, Carl Jung, Friedrich Nietzsche, Jordan Peterson, Aleksandr Solzhenitsyn, the Greek myths, the Scriptures, and the everyday realities of Nigerian life, Otito argues that the deepest struggles of humanity are rooted in the unseen life of the soul and the mind. Throughout the book, ordinary moments—a classroom, a football field, a family argument, a queue, a first kiss, a funeral, a heartbreak—become windows through which enduring truths about the human condition come into view.

On Becoming: Dust & Gold is an extended meditation on transformation. It explores the ways suffering can deform us or deepen us, the manner in which ghosts travel through generations until someone refuses to carry them any further, the resilience of hope in the face of disappointment, the defiance of writing against forgetting, and the lifelong tension between dust—the void from which we come—and gold—the possibility toward which we are continually becoming.

It asks them to confront the myths they have inherited, the stories they tell themselves, and the lives they have accepted.

Ultimately, On Becoming: Dust & Gold invites readers into a honest, no-holds-barred encounter with themselves. Becoming emerges as the lifelong labour of anyone willing to look inward with honesty, to wrestle with suffering without surrendering to it, and to keep reaching toward the person they were always capable of becoming.
